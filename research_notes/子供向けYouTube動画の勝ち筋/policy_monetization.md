# YouTube policy & monetization rules for "made for kids" (MFK) content, esp. AI-generated / mass-produced (state as of 2026-11-20)

> **Read this first — source-quality caveat.** In this research session, direct page fetches were blocked for every host tried (support.google.com, blog.youtube, tubefilter.com, digitaltrends.com, thenextweb.com). All findings below therefore come from **web-search result summaries**, not from reading the pages in full. Official YouTube Help URLs are cited where the search surfaced them, but **verbatim policy wording could not be re-read and confirmed**. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED-OFFICIAL]** are reported only by third-party blogs/aggregators. Items marked **[FROM MEMORY]** are my recollection of the official page text, not re-checked in this session. A report writer should treat exact numbers/wording as needing a final check against the live Help Center pages.

## 1. "Made for kids" designation (COPPA): disabled features and revenue impact

### Takeaway
MFK status removes personalized ads and most engagement/fan-funding features (comments, notifications, memberships, Super Chat, cards/end screens, etc.), and third-party datasets put kids RPM at the very bottom of all niches (median about $0.33 long-form in one ~300-channel study; CPM commonly quoted at $1–4). No kids-specific RPM figure for Japan was found.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube states that to comply with COPPA and other laws, certain features "may be restricted or turned off" for content set as made for kids; YouTube treats data from anyone watching an MFK video as coming from a child, regardless of the user's actual age. — [YouTube Help: Watching "made for kids" content / Features impacted by COPPA](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9632097?hl=en)
- Features listed as unavailable on MFK videos/channels across the official help page and coverage: personalized ads, comments, notifications, channel memberships, posts (Community), autoplay on home, cards and end screens, live chat, Super Chat / Super Stickers, donate button, merchandise and ticketing, video watermarks, mini player. The lists differ between 2020-era coverage and the current help page, so the live page should be checked for the current list. — [YouTube Help 9632097](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9632097?hl=en); [YouTube Help: Set your channel or video's audience](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654?hl=en); [Tubefilter 2020](https://www.tubefilter.com/2020/01/07/youtube-disables-more-product-features-kid-oriented-content/amp/)
- Third-party guide (2026) lists Super Thanks as also disabled on MFK, alongside Super Chat, Super Stickers, memberships, end screens, cards, comments and personalized ads. — [gyre.pro](https://gyre.pro/blog/how-to-monetize-a-youtube-kids-channel)
- Creators are required to label their content; under the FTC standard a video is MFK if it is intended for kids, considering a variety of factors. — [YouTube Help 9527654](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654?hl=en)
- Mislabeling carries real legal risk: Disney agreed to pay a $10 million civil penalty to settle FTC/DOJ allegations that it failed to label some YouTube videos as "Made for Kids", allowing data collection and targeted ads to under-13s. The FTC's root-cause finding was that Disney set audience at the **channel level** instead of checking each video. The order requires a program to review whether each video should be designated MFK. — [FTC business blog, Sept 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/09/protecting-children-watching-youtube-videos-lessons-learned-ftcs-settlement-disney); [Axios 2025-09-02](https://www.axios.com/2025/09/02/disney-ftc-settlement-lawsuit-youtube-video-children-privacy); [Malwarebytes, Jan 2026](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/privacy/2026/01/disney-fined-10m-for-mislabeling-kids-youtube-videos-and-violating-privacy-law)
- Kids & Teens had the lowest RPM of any niche in AIR Media-Tech's study of ~300 channels: **$0.33 median RPM** (May 2025–May 2026 sample, all countries; the source's own sample, not an official figure). — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-monetization-statistics-2026)
- Same source family reports a monetized playback rate of 12.1% for kids long-form vs 45.7% for kids Shorts. — [AIR Media-Tech Shorts RPM by niche](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/what-is-youtube-shorts-rpm-in-your-niche-in-2026)
- Blog-level claims: kids channels typically see CPM of $1–4 versus $8–20+ for comparable adult-audience content, attributed to no ad targeting and fewer ads served. — [fluxnote.io](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-earnings-by-country-comparison); [sozee.ai](https://www.sozee.ai/resources/?p=44275)
- Japan, **all content (not kids-specific)**: Dynamoi estimates creator RPM of about US$2.58 per 1,000 views (Sept 2025–Nov 2026), measured only on channels connected to Dynamoi. — [Dynamoi Japan RPM](https://dynamoi.com/de/data/youtube-adsense-rpm/jp)
- Standard long-form ad revenue split is 55% creator / 45% YouTube. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-cpm/); [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/za/blog/how-much-does-youtube-pay)

### Inferences
- With comments, notifications, memberships and Supers gone, an MFK channel's income is essentially contextual-ad revenue plus YouTube Premium share plus off-platform income (licensing, merchandise sold elsewhere, streaming music royalties). Volume of views, not RPM, is the lever.
- A Japanese-audience MFK channel would likely earn well below the ~$2.58 all-content Japan estimate; applying the global kids median ($0.33) is the only evidence-based planning anchor, and it is weak.
- Because of the Disney case, audience must be set **per video**, and a channel mixing kids and general content should not rely on a channel-level default.

### Gaps
- No source found with kids-specific RPM/CPM broken out by country (Japan vs US). All country data found is all-niche.
- The exact current official list of disabled features could not be read from the live help page (fetch blocked).
- 2025 COPPA Rule amendments: search did not return a source. **[FROM MEMORY, unverified]** the FTC finalized amendments in early 2025, effective mid-2025 with a compliance deadline around April 2026; verify on ftc.gov / Federal Register before citing.

## 2. YouTube Partner Program (YPP) requirements, state in 2026

### Takeaway
In 2026 the ad-revenue tier still needs 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours (12 months) or 10M Shorts views (90 days), but multiple secondary sources report YouTube announced in August 2026 that from **February 1, 2027** new applicants need 8,000 hours or 20M Shorts views. A channel starting now should plan against the higher bar.

### Cited Findings
- Two entry tiers: 500 subscribers for fan funding and Shopping features (in countries where the expanded program is available), or 1,000 subscribers for ad revenue sharing. — [YouTube Help: YPP overview & eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?rd=1); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/monetize-youtube-guide/)
- Current ad-revenue thresholds: 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours in the last 365 days or 10 million Shorts views in the last 90 days. — [streamer.guide](https://streamer.guide/blog/youtube-partner-program-2027-monetization-changes); [YouTube Help 72851](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?rd=1)
- **[UNVERIFIED-OFFICIAL]** Announced Aug 10–11, 2026: from Feb 1, 2027, new applicants need 1,000 subscribers plus **8,000 watch hours (365 days) or 20 million Shorts views (90 days)**; subscriber requirement unchanged; existing YPP members do not have to re-qualify but must accept updated terms by Jan 31, 2027. — [NewscastStudio 2026-08-10](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2026/08/10/youtube-partner-program-monetization-changes-2027/); [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/movies/youtube-just-doubled-the-grind-before-creators-can-start-making-ad-money/); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-partner-program-2027-changes/)
- **[UNVERIFIED, CONFLICTING]** Some guides claim that under the 2027 terms creators must maintain 10M qualified Shorts views per 90 days to keep earning from the Shorts pool; other guides do not mention this. — [creaticalc.com](https://creaticalc.com/blog/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027); not corroborated by [NewscastStudio](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2026/08/10/youtube-partner-program-monetization-changes-2027/)
- Inactivity: not uploading or posting for six months or more can cost monetization. — [YouTube Help 72851](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?rd=1)
- Channels that primarily target young audiences or are classified MFK must additionally comply with kids-specific monetization (quality) policies. — [YouTube Help: Best practices for kids & family content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223?hl=en)

### Inferences
- Because fan-funding features are disabled on MFK content, the 500-subscriber tier has little practical value for a pure kids channel; the 1,000-subscriber ad tier is the one that matters.
- A channel created in late 2026 is unlikely to qualify before Feb 1, 2027, so 8,000 hours / 20M Shorts views is the realistic target if the reported change is accurate.

### Gaps
- YouTube's own announcement of the 2027 change was not retrieved; the wording "qualified" hours/views used by several blogs suggests a definitional change that I could not verify.
- **[FROM MEMORY]** expanded-tier thresholds (500 subs + 3 public uploads in 90 days + 3,000 watch hours or 3M Shorts views) were not confirmed by this session's search results.

## 3. "Inauthentic content" (July 2025), reused content, AI/templated videos, 2026 enforcement

### Takeaway
AI itself is not banned or demonetized; what loses monetization (and in 2026, whole channels) is mass-produced, templated, near-identical output. 2026 brought real enforcement: a January purge of 16 big AI channels and, in February, YPP suspension of AI kids channels named by a New York Times investigation.

### Cited Findings
**July 15, 2025 update**
- YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" monetization rule to "inauthentic content" effective July 15, 2025, describing it as a clarification to better identify mass-produced and repetitious content, not a new policy. Examples given: channels uploading narrated stories with only superficial differences, and slideshows that all have the same narration. — [TechCrunch 2025-07-09](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/09/youtube-prepares-crackdown-on-mass-produced-and-repetitive-videos-as-concern-over-ai-slop-grows); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)
- YouTube said the change was a "minor update" not specifically aimed at AI (creator liaison Rene Ritchie's video); AI tools remain welcome if rules are followed. — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/youtubes-ai-slop-crackdown-has-creators-concerned-marketers-cheering); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/de/news/youtube-responds-to-ai-concerns/)
- **[FROM MEMORY of official text, not re-read]** the policy defines inauthentic content as mass-produced or repetitive content, including content that looks made from a template with little to no variation across videos, or that is easily replicable at scale. — [YouTube Help: channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)

**Reused content (separate rule)**
- Reused content = repurposing material already on YouTube or elsewhere without significant original commentary, substantive modification, or educational/entertainment value; it applies even with the original creator's permission. — [exemplary.ai summary of policy](https://exemplary.ai/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy); official page: [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Reported policy examples: not allowed — content that exclusively features readings of materials you did not create; songs altered only in pitch/speed. Allowed — using AI to visualize a character and story you invented, using AI to edit your scripts. An AI/cloned voice narrating an original script is reported as not a violation. — [ytgrowth.io](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/youtube-ai-policy); [flashtts.com](https://www.flashtts.com/blog/is-ai-voice-allowed-on-youtube-monetization)
- Inauthentic content is described as covering image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal narrative, commentary, or educational value. — [vidmyth.com](https://www.vidmyth.com/guides/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy)

**2026 updates and enforcement**
- **[UNVERIFIED-OFFICIAL]** July 16, 2026: YouTube reportedly published clarified YPP guidance sorting inauthentic content into three categories: (1) generic / template-based / repetitive AI output, (2) "unsatisfying or off-putting" content (also described as content built to distress or manipulate), (3) AI personas giving advice on sensitive topics (health, finance, legal). Described as a reorganization, not an AI ban; consequence is monetization eligibility rather than video removal. Category wording varies between blogs. — [outlierkit.com](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-updates-july-2026); [versely.studio](https://www.versely.studio/blog/inauthentic-content-is-the-policy-that-decides-whether-ai-video-earns); [channelterminated.com](https://channelterminated.com/demonetized/inauthentic-content/)
- January 2026: YouTube terminated or wiped 16 AI channels with about 35 million subscribers and 4.7 billion lifetime views (11 fully terminated, 5 had content wiped); estimated ~US$10M annual ad revenue combined. YouTube said channels were terminated for violating spam policies. Followed a Kapwing study. — [Tubefilter 2026-01-29](https://tubefilter.com/2026/01/29/youtube-ai-slop-channel-crackdown-bans/); [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/youtube-cracks-down-ai-generated-content-1800213); [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2589917/youtube-deletes-47-billion-views-worth-of-ai-spam-videos-in-major-platform-crackdown)
- Kapwing analysis: roughly 21% of the first 500 videos recommended to a new user were low-quality AI content. — [Tubefilter 2026-01-29](https://tubefilter.com/2026/01/29/youtube-ai-slop-channel-crackdown-bans/)
- February 2026 (AI kids channels): after a New York Times investigation into AI-generated videos for toddlers/preschoolers, YouTube suspended all five cited channels from the Partner Program; they can no longer earn ad revenue or appear on YouTube Kids. YouTube also removed three hyperrealistic videos from the Kids app and took down one clip for violating child safety policies. The investigation found ~40% of Shorts recommended after kids' content contained AI visuals. — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/social-media/investigation-finds-youtube-is-serving-mindless-ai-slop-to-toddlers-and-preschoolers/); [Dexerto 2026-02-27](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-strikes-down-channels-after-investigation-into-bizarre-ai-content-for-kids-3326231/)
- June 2026 reporting says the crackdown is also hitting legitimate faceless creators as collateral damage; YouTube maintains AI-assisted content is not penalized for carrying an AI label, and voiceover alone does not trigger demonetization. — [The Next Web 2026-06-15](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage); [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/youtubes-war-on-ai-slop-collides-with-faceless-creators-who-built-the-platform-first/)
- **[UNVERIFIED — low reliability]** a claim of "130,000 channels" purged circulates on an aggregator; not confirmed against YouTube. — [agenticbrew.ai](https://www.agenticbrew.ai/news/0a65f490-d43b-46dd-b765-79ab3ab05c71/youtube-s-130-000-channel-ai-slop-purge)

### Inferences
- Code-generated animation is exactly the kind of output that can look "made with a template with little to no variation" if every video shares the same layout, voice, pacing and structure with only a word or colour swapped. The risk is in sameness across the catalogue, not in the tool used.
- Practical risk reducers implied by the policy examples: original scripts, distinct story/learning goal per episode, visible variation in visuals and structure, a consistent original character/world (the "AI to visualize a character and story you invented" example), and a modest upload cadence rather than bulk uploads.
- Enforcement has two severities: monetization loss (inauthentic content, a YPP policy) and termination (spam policy, a Community Guideline). The January 2026 terminations were under the latter, so extreme mass-production can cost the channel, not just revenue.
- Review happens at channel level at YPP application and again later, so a channel that passes initially can still be suspended.

### Gaps
- Could not read the official inauthentic/reused content text or confirm the July 16, 2026 three-category wording from YouTube directly.
- Names of the five NYT-cited kids channels and the 16 January channels were not captured in search summaries.
- No official statement found on how many videos/what similarity level triggers the policy; upload-volume thresholds quoted by blogs are anecdotes.

## 4. Quality principles for kids & family content, child safety policy, Elsagate-type content

### Takeaway
Kids channels face an extra monetization gate: a channel with a "strong focus" on low-quality MFK content can be suspended from YPP, and individual low-quality videos get limited or no ads. Separately, the child safety Community Guideline bans mature themes dressed up as family content (the Elsagate pattern), which leads to removal and strikes rather than just demonetization.

### Cited Findings
- Low-quality MFK principles: heavily commercial or promotional; encouraging negative behaviors or attitudes; deceptively educational; hindering comprehension; sensational or misleading. — [YouTube Help: Best practices for kids & family content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223?hl=en); [TechCrunch 2021-10-25](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/25/youtube-warns-creators-it-will-start-to-demonetize-low-quality-kids-videos-next-month)
- "Heavily promotional" is defined as content mainly featuring products, brands and logos (toys, food) or encouraging excessive buying, e.g. unboxing videos. — [YouTube Help 10774223](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223?hl=en)
- Consequences: a channel found to have a strong focus on low-quality MFK content may be suspended from YPP; individual videos violating the principles may receive limited or no ads; low-quality content also gets reduced recommendations. Enforcement began November 2021. — [YouTube Help 10774223](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223?hl=en); [YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/enabling-high-quality-youtube-kids-experience/); [Tubefilter 2021-10-26](https://tubefilter.com/2021/10/26/youtube-curtail-monetization-heavily-commercial-videos/)
- These principles apply to channels primarily targeting young audiences or classified as MFK. — [YouTube Help 10774223](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223?hl=en)
- **[FROM MEMORY of official text, not re-read]** the high-quality principles are: being a good person; learning and inspiring curiosity; creativity, play and imagination; interaction with real-world issues; diversity, equity and inclusion. A sixth low-quality principle is "strange use of children's characters". — [YouTube Help 10774223](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223?hl=en); [PDF version](https://services.google.com/fh/files/helpcenter/best_practices_for_kids_and_family_content_en.pdf)
- Child safety policy: "misleading family content" that includes mature themes packaged as family-friendly or made for kids is prohibited under Community Guidelines. — [Google Transparency Report: child safety](https://transparencyreport.google.com/youtube-policy/featured-policies/child-safety?hl=en_GB); official policy page: [YouTube Help: Child safety policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801999?hl=en)
- Labeling adult-themed content as MFK can itself be removed as misleading family content. — [channelterminated.com](https://channelterminated.com/channel-types/kids-content/)
- In the February 2026 AI-kids case, one clip was removed specifically for violating child safety policies, showing the policy is applied to AI kids content. — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-strikes-down-channels-after-investigation-into-bizarre-ai-content-for-kids-3326231/)

### Inferences
- "Deceptively educational" and "hindering comprehension" are the two principles most likely to catch AI/code-generated kids videos: titles promising learning with no real instruction, garbled or nonsensical narration, incoherent visuals, wrong letters/numbers/facts from generative models.
- "Strange use of children's characters" plus the child-safety policy means using known IP characters (Elsa, Spider-Man, Peppa etc.) in odd scenarios is a double risk: copyright and child safety.
- Factual accuracy checks (spelling, counting, colours, animal names) and a clear age-appropriate learning goal per video are the cheapest protection against the quality-principle gate.

### Gaps
- Could not read the full child safety policy text, examples, or strike rules (fetch blocked). **[FROM MEMORY]** standard Community Guideline enforcement is a warning, then three strikes in 90 days leading to termination; severe child-safety violations can terminate without warning. Verify on the official page.
- No official statement found that the quality principles were revised in 2025–2026 to mention AI explicitly.

## 5. AI disclosure (altered/synthetic content label) and AI-specific kids rules

### Takeaway
Disclosure is required for realistic AI content; clearly unrealistic animation is exempt, so stylized code/AI animation generally does not need the label. There is no confirmed YouTube rule specifically banning or restricting AI-generated MFK content on main YouTube, but YouTube says it limits AI content in the YouTube Kids app to a small set of high-quality channels.

### Cited Findings
- Disclosure is required for realistic altered/synthetic content and meaningful changes; not required for clearly unrealistic content or minor edits. Official examples of exempt content include someone riding a unicorn through a fantastical world and an AI-generated animation of a missile in a fully animated video. — [YouTube Help: Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)
- A disclosure label alone does not change how a video is recommended or whether it can earn money. — [YouTube blog: Improving AI labels](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/)
- May 2026 update: labels made more prominent with a single label format for photorealistic and meaningfully AI altered/generated content; YouTube began automatically detecting and labeling AI videos even without creator disclosure. For unrealistic or animated content, disclosure appears in the expanded description rather than on the player. — [YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/); [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/youtube-will-now-automatically-detect-and-label-ai-videos/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-introduces-more-prominent-ai-labels/821305/)
- Cartoons are largely exempt from the disclosure requirement because the rule turns on whether content "seems realistic". — [Undark 2026-03-20](https://undark.org/2026/03/20/ai-slop-children/)
- April 1, 2026: Fairplay-led open letter from 200+ experts/organizations to Neal Mohan and Sundar Pichai made four requests: label all AI content, ban AI content from YouTube Kids, no algorithmic recommendation of AI content to under-18s, and a parental toggle to turn it off. — [Tubefilter 2026-04-01](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/04/01/youtube-fairplay-kids-ai-open-letter/); [Fairplay letter PDF](https://fairplayforkids.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/YouTube-Letter-AI-Slop.pdf); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/414000/google-pressured-to-stop-serving-ai-video-to-kids.html)
- YouTube's response: it limits AI-generated content in the YouTube Kids app to a small set of high-quality channels, parents can block channels, and it is working on AI labels for YouTube Kids (no timeline). — [NBC](https://www.nbcmiami.com/news/national-international/advocacy-groups-urge-youtube-protect-kids-ai-slop-videos/3789597/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/414000/google-pressured-to-stop-serving-ai-video-to-kids.html)
- As of Oct 20, 2026, a third-party guide reports YouTube Kids help pages mention no AI switch or AI label. — [kidsaitools.com](https://www.kidsaitools.com/en/articles/ai-generated-videos-youtube-kids-parents)

### Inferences
- A new AI-animated channel should assume it will **not** be admitted to the YouTube Kids app in the near term, given YouTube's stated limit to a small set of high-quality channels. Main-YouTube MFK viewing is the realistic distribution.
- Voluntary disclosure is low-cost (label does not affect monetization per YouTube) and protects against the auto-detection system labeling the video anyway. Photorealistic AI kids video is the riskiest style: it triggers the label and was the type removed from YouTube Kids in February 2026.
- Regulatory and advocacy pressure is rising; rules specific to AI kids content could tighten at short notice.

### Gaps
- No official text found on whether a synthetic (TTS/cloned) voice alone requires disclosure in an animated video. **[FROM MEMORY]** cloning a real person's voice requires disclosure; generic TTS narration does not. Verify.
- No confirmed announcement of an AI label or AI parental toggle shipping on YouTube Kids.
- Penalties for non-disclosure could not be read from the official page.

## 6. YouTube Kids app: inclusion and eligibility

### Takeaway
Marking a video MFK does not put it in YouTube Kids. Inclusion is decided by YouTube through automated filters, user feedback and human review; no creator application process was found.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube uses a mix of algorithmic filtering, user input and human review to decide what is available in YouTube Kids. — [YouTube Kids Help: What videos are available](https://support.google.com/youtubekids/answer/6172307?hl=en)
- Videos set as MFK are not automatically included in the YouTube Kids app. — [YouTube Help: FAQ about "made for kids"](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9684541); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-kids-content-filtering-explained-how-videos-pass-the-gate/)
- Homescreen videos get an extra layer of human review; a "channel review" includes human confirmation of many videos on the channel. — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-kids-content-filtering-explained-how-videos-pass-the-gate/)
- YouTube Kids content policies: content must be family-friendly; videos with paid product placements or endorsements are not allowed; overly commercial content (e.g. focus on product packaging, directly encouraging children to spend) and deceptive, sensational or clickbait content are excluded. — [YouTube Help: Content policies for YouTube Kids](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10938174?hl=en); [YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/our-responsibility-approach-protecting-kids-and-families-youtube/)
- YouTube acknowledges automated systems are imperfect and it cannot manually review everything. — [YouTube Kids Help 6172307](https://support.google.com/youtubekids/answer/6172307?hl=en)
- Channels suspended from YPP in the Feb 2026 AI-kids case also lost eligibility to appear on YouTube Kids. — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/social-media/investigation-finds-youtube-is-serving-mindless-ai-slop-to-toddlers-and-preschoolers/)
- AI-generated content in the app is limited to a small set of high-quality channels (YouTube spokesperson, April 2026). — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/414000/google-pressured-to-stop-serving-ai-video-to-kids.html)

### Inferences
- Paid product placement makes a video ineligible for YouTube Kids, so sponsorship income and Kids-app distribution are mutually exclusive per video.
- Since creators cannot apply, the only levers are correct MFK labeling, meeting the quality principles, and avoiding commercial or clickbait signals.

### Gaps
- No official statement found confirming or denying a creator-side application/appeal route for YouTube Kids inclusion; vidIQ's description of selection signals is self-described speculation ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/tr/blog/post/youtube-kids-app)).

## 7. Music / copyright: public-domain nursery rhymes, AI music, Content ID

### Takeaway
Evidence here is the weakest in this report: only secondary blogs, which disagree. The consistent points are that monetizing your own video with AI music is generally allowed if the generator's licence grants commercial use, that you cannot reliably enrol AI tracks in Content ID yourself, and that claims mostly arise when someone else registered a similar track first.

### Cited Findings
- Monetizing your own videos with Suno music is reported as allowed only on Suno Pro/Premier and only for tracks generated during an active paid subscription. — [sunowatermarkremover.com](https://sunowatermarkremover.com/youtube/); [makebestmusic.com](https://makebestmusic.com/blog/can-you-monetize-suno-ai-music-on-youtube)
- Main claim risk: another creator generates a similar track from similar prompts and registers it with Content ID first; two tracks from comparable prompts can be very similar. — [replayedstudio.com](https://replayedstudio.com/blog/ai-music-content-id/); [undetectr.com](https://undetectr.com/blog/ai-music-content-id)
- You generally cannot register an AI-generated track as your own Content ID reference because Content ID requires exclusive rights. — [blog.genxnotes.com](https://blog.genxnotes.com/en/can-you-get-youtube-content-id-on-suno-generated-ai-music/)
- **[CONFLICTING / UNVERIFIED]** One guide says a July 2025 YouTube policy made "fully AI-generated music (audio-only, unmodified)" ineligible for monetization and Content ID; another says Content ID neither detects nor bans AI. Neither was confirmed against a YouTube primary source. — [outlierkit.com](https://outlierkit.com/resources/ai-generated-music-youtube-monetization-2026/) vs [dev.to](https://dev.to/akaranjkar08/suno-songs-on-youtube-2026-content-id-and-disclosure-40p7)
- US position reported: purely AI-generated works are not copyrightable. — [hookgenius.app](https://hookgenius.app/learn/suno-legal-guide/)
- Shorts using licensed music share part of their revenue with music licensing; Shorts without music send all associated revenue to the creator pool. — [TechCrunch 2023-01-09](https://techcrunch.com/2023/01/09/youtube-new-partner-program-terms-shorts-revenue-sharing-february-1/)

### Inferences
- For traditional nursery rhymes, the composition (melody/lyrics) may be public domain but any specific **recording or arrangement** is separately owned; using someone else's recording will draw claims. Creating one's own recording/arrangement (human or licensed-AI) is the safe path. This is general copyright reasoning, not something a source in this session confirmed.
- False or overlapping Content ID claims on public-domain melodies are plausible because many parties register near-identical arrangements; keep project files, generation logs and licence/subscription receipts to dispute claims.
- Avoid "modern classics" that are still in copyright in some territories; check each song's status individually, and separately for Japan (different copyright term rules, JASRAC administration).

### Gaps
- No source found that specifically addresses Content ID claims on public-domain nursery rhymes or the public-domain status of specific songs.
- No official YouTube source retrieved on AI-music monetization or Content ID eligibility.
- Japan-specific music rights (JASRAC/NexTone handling on YouTube, status of Japanese children's songs / doyo) not researched.

## 8. Shorts for kids: monetization and viability

### Takeaway
Kids Shorts can earn from the Shorts revenue pool, but at extremely low RPM (roughly $0.02–0.11 per 1,000 views in third-party data). They appear more useful as a discovery and volume channel than as income, and kids Shorts are also where AI-slop scrutiny is highest.

### Cited Findings
- Shorts ad revenue is pooled and allocated among eligible creators; creators keep 45% of their allocated share (model in place since Feb 1, 2023). — [TechCrunch 2023-01-09](https://techcrunch.com/2023/01/09/youtube-new-partner-program-terms-shorts-revenue-sharing-february-1/); [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/youtube-shorts-monetization)
- Kids & Teens Shorts RPM in 2026: $0.05–$0.11 per 1,000 views in one dataset (274 channels). — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/what-is-youtube-shorts-rpm-in-your-niche-in-2026)
- Another analysis reports $0.02–$0.05 Shorts RPM for small and medium kids channels. — [gyre.pro](https://gyre.pro/blog/how-to-monetize-a-youtube-kids-channel)
- Kids Shorts had a 45.7% monetized playback rate versus 12.1% for kids long-form in the AIR sample. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/what-is-youtube-shorts-rpm-in-your-niche-in-2026)
- Among large kids channels posting 21+ Shorts a month, Shorts brought 41% of revenue. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-shorts-rpm-vs-long-form-how-much-do-shorts-earn-in-2026)
- Scrutiny: the NYT investigation found ~40% of Shorts recommended after kids' content contained AI visuals; Kapwing found kids Shorts showed even higher low-quality-AI exposure than the 21% general figure. — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/social-media/investigation-finds-youtube-is-serving-mindless-ai-slop-to-toddlers-and-preschoolers/); [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/01/29/youtube-ai-slop-channel-crackdown-bans/)

### Inferences
- At $0.05–0.11 RPM, 10 million kids Shorts views yields roughly $500–1,100; 10 million long-form views at the $0.33 median yields about $3,300. Long-form (and long watch sessions, compilations) is the better revenue format; Shorts mainly help reach the Shorts-view YPP threshold.
- If the reported Feb 2027 threshold (20M Shorts views / 90 days) and any Shorts-pool maintenance floor are real, a Shorts-only kids strategy becomes considerably harder.
- Mass-posting near-identical AI kids Shorts sits squarely in the pattern targeted in 2026 enforcement.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube source found stating any MFK-specific restriction on Shorts features or Shorts monetization; whether MFK Shorts are treated differently in the Shorts feed or pool is unconfirmed.
- All Shorts RPM figures are from vendor datasets (AIR Media-Tech, gyre.pro) with undisclosed methodology and country mix.
